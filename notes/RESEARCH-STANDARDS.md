# Research Standards

Working criteria for what counts as a good reference source when we
research a topic for this project (component types, physical principles,
circuit techniques, etc.) and want to link it as supporting material.

## The bar

A source is worth citing when it:

1. **Is written for a technical beginner.** Approachable entry point —
   doesn't assume prior EE coursework, defines terms as it goes.
2. **Uses illustrations where they help.** Diagrams, labeled parts,
   comparison charts — the kind of visual that makes a physical
   mechanism click, not just decorative images.
3. **Doesn't gloss over the physical/technical reality.** Beginner-
   friendly is not the same as dumbed-down. A good source explains the
   real underlying mechanism (e.g. *why* a capacitor charges fast and a
   battery doesn't — the electrostatic vs. chemical distinction) rather
   than papering over it with an analogy that breaks down under a
   follow-up question.

## In practice

- Prefer a **pair of sources** when possible: one aimed at general/
  beginner audiences (confirms the conceptual model is right and
  accessible) and one aimed at an engineering audience (confirms nothing
  got oversimplified into being wrong). Cross-checking two independent
  sources this way also guards against one article just being wrong.
- Reputable, established educational sources (e.g. SparkFun Learn,
  Electronics Notes, vendor engineering blogs from component
  manufacturers/distributors, Wikipedia for background/definitions) are
  preferred over SEO content-farm articles that recycle the same
  shallow paragraph across many domains.
- A chart or figure (e.g. a Ragone plot for energy vs. power density) is
  worth linking directly when it's the clearest way to see a tradeoff,
  even if the surrounding article is just decent rather than great.
- When research turns into a written summary (as with
  [CAPACITORS.md](CAPACITORS.md)), keep the source links attached so the
  underlying claims can be checked or re-derived later — the summary is
  not a replacement for being able to go verify it.

This standard applies to any topic we research going forward, not just
capacitors — it's the working definition of "good research" for this
project.
